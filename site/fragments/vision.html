02 · PLANNED · PORTABLE HIERARCHY

Project coordination

One project, two dependent milestones, one explicit commitment. Every node names its scope version, expected artifact and acceptance criteria; every suggestion explains itself.

What the screen answers

Who holds which role, what each node expects as an artifact, which dependency gates which milestone — and where an agreement has actually been made, versus where only an offer or a matching suggestion exists.

Criteria addressed
Stable identifiers and scope versions · expected artifact and acceptance criteria per node · dependencies must not silently expand scope.

Offer and commitment are different states

An offer is one participant's input; a commitment binds participants to a scope version, review responsibility and change terms. The outline card never looks like the bound one.

Criteria addressed
Offers recorded separately from commitments · publishing a card assigns no task · matching explains why it suggested, without claiming verification.

03 · PLANNED · TWO VERIFICATION PATHS

Artifact receipts

One delivery manifest, two deliberately separate checks: a content identifier addresses content; a raw-file digest checks the delivered bytes. Verification states read as evidence, never as decoration.

CIDcontent address

Names content and encoding. Establishes which bytes this manifest refers to. Does not establish quality, authorship, rights or acceptance.

raw digestbyte check

SHA-256 over the retrieved bytes. Establishes that what someone downloaded matches this manifest — independently, by anyone with retrieval access.

matched mismatch absent stale revision four states a delivery can be in — matched bytes of a superseded revision are still not a current delivery

Criteria addressed
Detect mismatched bytes, unsafe references, absent content and stale revisions · distinguish a CID from a raw-file digest · retention and access conditions belong to the receipt.

04 · PLANNED · BOUND DECISION TRAIL

Acceptance & QA

The proposed flow — delivery, revision request, new delivery, acceptance — as one portable trail. Every decision binds the exact delivery and criteria versions; a stale approval is refused, not silently superseded.

  1. Delivery rev 1

    A contributor delivers a manifest and evidence.

  2. Revision requested

    The QA role names the missing evidence; the decision binds rev 1 × criteria v3.

  3. Delivery rev 2

    A new version — nothing inherited from the revision request.

  4. Accepted

    Coordinator and QA both bind rev 2 × criteria v3. Settlement, if separately enabled, consumes its own authorization — a delivery is never permission to pay.

What refusal looks like

An approval bound to rev 1 is refused: stale version. An approval from the delivery's author is refused: role. Duplicate decisions add nothing; an absent reviewer opens the named dispute path.

What the screen makes visible

Authorization at the decision point: every decision names its actor and role as rendered facts. Independence is concrete — a second account alone is not an independent reviewer.

Criteria addressed
Stale-version and duplicate decision rejection · authorization checked at the decision point · no self-granted reviewer authority.

05 · RESEARCH · TESTNET TARGET

Solidity contract lab

A guided builder that starts from explicit requirements — roles, scope commitments, artifact references, acceptance thresholds, deadlines — and produces readable Solidity together with the normalized agreement, assumptions, threat model and reproducible tests. Signing authority binds action, scope, delivery version, recipient, expiry and a single-use nonce.

What the lab produces

A pinned, reproducible package: contract, agreement, assumptions, threat model, version-pinned dependencies, compiler settings and tests — with administrative power visible in the agreement and interface.

Criteria addressed
Pinned reproducible output · role, signature, replay, nonce and expiry tests · signing authority separate from Commons login.

What the lab refuses

No private-key collection, no automatic signing, no implicit deployment, no unprompted financial code. First slice: a local example with synthetic artifacts and no real funds; any testnet exercise needs separate authorization.

Criteria addressed
Clear review of the requested operation · a testnet demonstration is not a real-value launch.

06 · PROPOSED · OPTIONAL

Fair settlement

The agreement — budget, asset, contributors, fees, funding responsibility, release conditions, rights, deadlines, cancellation and dispute path — comes before the work. An optional independent instance holds one agreement's funds within its accepted authority; voluntary work remains voluntary.

funded delivered accepted disputed released refunded cancelled every outcome defined before implementation — a delivery reference alone releases nothing

The gate before real value

A published threat model, an independently reviewed implementation with resolved findings, fund invariants and adversarial tests, bytecode/source correspondence, and transparent administrative powers — including their absence.

Criteria addressed
Conservation of funds and bounded release/refund · forged, replayed and front-run decisions exercised · incident, pause and recovery procedures.
